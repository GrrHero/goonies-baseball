Fielding

Ready Position

Popcorn Cue word — say only this while they move.

Why it matters

Every play starts here. A good ready position means kids can move to the ball instead of reacting late from a standstill.

How to

  1. Feet wider than shoulders, weight on the balls of the feet.
  2. Knees bent, hips down — like sitting on a low stool.
  3. Glove open and out in front; throwing hand nearby.
  4. Eyes up on the batter / ball — chest stays tall.

Common mistakes

Standing straight up with hands at the sides
Cue "Popcorn!" and have them drop until their glove almost touches the grass.
Bending only at the waist (hunched over)
Ask "Can you still see my shirt logo?" Eyes up keeps the chest up.
Feet together or crossed when they shuffle
"Don't let your shoes kiss." Practice slow side-shuffles first.

Coach station drill

Practice · 4–5 min Popcorn Freeze

Setup: Open grass. Whole team jogs or skips in a 15×15 area.

How: Coach claps and yells "Popcorn!" Kids freeze in ready position for 2 seconds, then jog again. Do 8–10 pops. Fake a clap sometimes — kids who don't bite get a cheer, not an out. Keep lines nonexistent: everyone moves at once.

10-minute at-home drill

Home · 10 min Living Room Popcorn

Gear: Glove (or bare hands), open floor space

How: Parent claps "Popcorn!" while the child jogs in place or around the room. Hold ready 2 seconds. Add: after the pop, point left/right for two shuffle steps. Finish with 5 quiet "statue" holds.

Level up

🚀 After popping, shuffle two steps the way you point, then pop again — without standing up first.
