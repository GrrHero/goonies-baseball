Sample 60-minute practice

Freeze signal

Pick one signal for the whole season (both hands up, or a long whistle). When it happens: feet stop, eyes on coach, mouths quiet. Practice it once every practice like a drill — not only when kids are wild.

Praise language that works

  • Praise the cue: “That was a great Chomp — glove ate grass!”
  • Praise effort and hustle louder than the highlight-reel play.
  • Be specific and short. Skip vague “good job” when you can name the action.
  • Public praise, private fix. Correct with the cue word while they move — not a lecture after.

Mixed skill levels & short attention

  • Stations beat lines. If more than 3 kids are waiting, split the group.
  • Same drill, different distance. Newer players closer; advanced kids step back or add Scoot.
  • One cue per activity. If you hear yourself saying a sentence, stop and say the word.
  • 90-second rule. Change the constraint (target, race, points) before boredom hits.
  • Helpers. Confident players can be “ball shaggers” or demo shadows — not permanent coaches.

Safety checklist

  • Helmets on in the batter’s box and on the bases.
  • Bats only in the on-deck / hitting circle — never in the dugout aisle.
  • Soft toss / front toss: foam or wiffle only; adult off the line of the swing.
  • No kids behind the throw target or under fly-ball paths during drills.
  • Water breaks every station; shade when heat is high.
  • Know who has the med kit and parent contacts before practice starts.
